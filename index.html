Scope and Motivation

The rapid wireless evolution towards 6G, coupled with its unprecedented convergence with AI and collaborative computing, is enabling a wide range of networked systems and complex applications. However, current communication and computing architectures are designed in isolation and operated under predefined goals, leading to amplified security risks and degraded effectiveness when systems scale up. Addressing these challenges requires intelligent orchestration of communication and computing resources through distributed collaboration, driving a paradigm shift from situation-agnostic, preconfigured systems to dynamic, goal-oriented operation that adapts to evolving user requirements. In this context, trust has emerged as a foundational mechanism for enabling dynamic collaboration and goal fulfillment by holistically assessing collaborators' capabilities and guiding coordinated, goal-oriented task execution in complex systems. Realizing this vision requires the development of trust- and intelligence-native networks and systems, where trust and intelligence are intrinsically embedded within system architecture, operational logic, and dynamic collaboration. The Workshop on Trust- and Intelligence-Native Networks and Systems (TINS) invites cutting-edge research on new concepts, models, and applications related to trust and intelligence in future systems.

Topics of Interest

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • System modeling, goal-oriented optimization and operation of networks
  • Situation-awareness through collaborative sensing and computing
  • Protocols for trust- and intelligence-native networks and systems
  • Trust- and intelligence-native architectures for future networks
  • Task-specific trust modeling
  • Dynamic and evolving trust modeling in large-scale networks
  • Trust evaluation under uncertainty and incomplete information
  • Trust-aware routing, access control, and resource management
  • Integrated trust, security, and privacy mechanisms
  • Interpretability, explainability, and transparency in trust modeling
  • Trust-aware collaborator selection and coordination
  • Intelligent task decomposition and dynamic collaboration formation
  • Collective intelligence for goal-oriented network operation
  • Task-oriented intelligence orchestration and decision-making
  • Distributed collaboration among resource-constrained devices
  • Explainable and trustworthy AI for future networks
  • Embodied AI / Agentic AI for intelligence-native systems
  • Physical AI for spatial intelligence systems
  • Evaluation metrics for trust- and intelligence-native systems
  • Testbeds and real-world deployments
